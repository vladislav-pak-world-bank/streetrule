Rental housing law, one address at a time

Which housing rules bind this building today?

State statutes, city ordinances and pending bills overlap. StreetRule finds the legal city, checks each rule against the building, and quotes the source. When the record cannot decide, the answer is unknown, and it names the fact that would settle it.

    Not legal advice. A research prototype on the RealPage challenge corpus: 500 sample addresses, law as of 1 Oct 2026.

    Three ways a rule lookup goes wrong

    A city name matched to a list of laws. Each mistake below changes the answer for real buildings in the sample.

    1. The mailing city is not the legal city

      Dorchester, Roxbury and Allston are Boston. San Ysidro is San Diego. A name match sends the tenant to the wrong city's law.

    2. Coverage hides in the fine print

      San Francisco rent control stops at buildings first certified after 13 June 1979. California's cap exempts buildings under 15 years old. Those thresholds are read as numbers and checked.

    3. A bill is not a law, and dates disagree

      New Jersey's FAIR Act is enacted and starts in 2027. Massachusetts's algorithm bills have not passed. Berkeley's ban has two published start dates.

    How an answer is made

    The model reads. Arithmetic decides. Method and evidence

    Read the law

    Each supplied document is read into a strict schema. A rule is kept only when its quote appears word for word in the source.

    Extract the thresholds

    Coverage text becomes cutoff dates, unit limits, and owner-occupied and new-construction exemptions.

    Find the legal city

    The US Census Geocoder returns the incorporated place. A match to a different house number is rejected.

    Decide, and show the work

    Each rule is applies, unknown, superseded, not yet in force, or a bill. Every check is listed under the quote.
